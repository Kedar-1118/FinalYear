\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Tabular prediction benchmarks across scientific and enterprise domains remain predominantly governed by recursive tree-based architectures, including Random Forests \cite{breiman2001random} and Gradient Boosted Decision Trees \cite{ke2017lightgbm,chen2016xgboost}. While such ensembles provide inherent scale invariance, native handling of categorical features, and structural interpretability, inducting deep tree topologies over massive instance volumes ($N \ge 10^6$) incurs an acute computational toll.

The dominant computational bottleneck during tree induction resides within internal node split optimization. Greedily partitioning a node containing $N$ training instances across $D$ continuous predictors requires evaluating impurity reductions (such as Gini Gain or entropy delta) across candidate split thresholds. Canonical implementations (e.g., CART \cite{breiman1984classification}) sort instance values along every dimension, demanding $\mathcal{O}(D \cdot N \log N)$ operations per split. Although histogram binning (as implemented in LightGBM and FastBDT) reduces continuous coordinates to $B$ discrete buckets and computes split gains in $\mathcal{O}(D \cdot (N + B))$ time, iterating through all $N$ partition residents for every candidate $(j, \theta)$ pair continues to dominate end-to-end training latency as datasets scale.

\subsection{Bandit-Based Split Identification: Potentials and Impediments}
To untie node-splitting cost from the raw sample cardinality $N$, recent formulations frame threshold selection as a Best Arm Identification (BAI) pure exploration game in Multi-Armed Bandits \cite{tiwari2022mabsplit,maron1994hoeffding}. In this paradigm, each candidate split threshold represents an arm whose stochastic reward reflects the empirical impurity reduction estimated over a drawn minibatch of size $M \ll N$. By evaluating arms sequentially and tracking statistical upper and lower confidence intervals, provably sub-optimal splits are discarded before scanning the entire partition population.

Nevertheless, existing bandit-driven splitters encounter four fundamental structural and probabilistic deficiencies:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Superpopulation Mismatch under Without-Replacement Subsampling:} Prior formulations employ standard Hoeffding or empirical Bernstein bounds that presume independent, identically distributed (i.i.d.) draws from an infinite superpopulation. However, decision tree training partitions a fixed, finite sample collection $\mathcal{D}$ without replacement. Applying i.i.d. bounds under without-replacement sampling introduces loose confidence intervals and artificial termination delays, failing to collapse uncertainty to zero even when all $N$ available node samples are observed.
    \item \textbf{High-Dimensional Cache Saturation under Uniform Binning:} Evaluating all $K = D \times B$ candidate split points at fine quantization ($B = 256$) from the opening iteration introduces severe memory bandwidth churn, spending substantial CPU cycles pulling samples for features that lack predictive signal.
    \item \textbf{Static Minibatch Sizing throughout Arm Rejection:} Conventional implementations maintain a static batch step $M_0$ across elimination rounds. Continuing to query broad minibatches when the active arm candidate set has pruned from thousands down to two contenders wastes memory bandwidth during the terminal separation phase.
    \item \textbf{Bookkeeping Penalties on Shallow Subtrees:} Deep in the tree hierarchy, child partitions inevitably dwindle ($N < 500$). At this terminal scale, maintaining active arm arrays, updating empirical variances, and calculating logarithmic radii consumes more CPU cycles than directly executing a SIMD-vectorized exact sort.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Our Contributions}
To resolve these deficiencies, we propose \textbf{MABSplit}, an accelerated tree and forest induction engine grounded in finite-population concentration bounds and multi-stage arm scheduling:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Finite-Population Serfling Empirical Bernstein Concentration:} We derive variance-adaptive concentration bounds that incorporate the finite population correction (FPC) coefficient $(1 - (n-1)/N)$. This guarantees that confidence radii contract strictly to zero when node samples are fully observed, eliminating tie-breaking stalling while rigorously preserving $(\epsilon, \delta)$-PAC optimality.
    \item \textbf{Hierarchical Coarse-to-Fine Arm Screening (H-MAB):} We introduce a two-tier screening pipeline that first prunes unpromising dimensions at 16-bin quantization under an allocated Bonferroni budget $\delta_{\text{coarse}} = \delta/3$, subsequently refining candidate arms to 256-bin precision with $\delta_{\text{fine}} = 2\delta/3$, provably preserving the global $(\epsilon, \delta)$-PAC guarantee.
    \item \textbf{Adaptive Dynamic Minibatch Allocation (ADMA):} We develop a sample allocation schedule that scales minibatch size $M_t$ dynamically as a function of the active arm cardinality $|\mathcal{A}_t|$, saving up to 30\% of total sample queries during the terminal phase of arm selection.
    \item \textbf{Depth-Adaptive Hybrid Solver:} We formulate an automatic dispatch mechanism governed by a critical node size threshold $N_{\text{thresh}}$, seamlessly delegating shallow sub-trees to SIMD-vectorized exact sorting and large nodes to the accelerated MAB engine.
    \item \textbf{Empirical Split Fidelity and Throughput Benchmarks:} Across synthetic and real-world tabular benchmarks (Adult, Covertype, HIGGS), MABSplit delivers a \textbf{3.2$\times$--11.8$\times$ wall-clock speedup} over exact CART and outperforms histogram gradient boosters while guaranteeing $>99.1\%$ split agreement and matching baseline generalization accuracy.
\end{itemize}
