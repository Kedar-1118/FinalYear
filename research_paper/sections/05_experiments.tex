\section{Experimental Evaluation}
\label{sec:experiments}

We structure our empirical inquiry around four core investigative hypotheses:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{RQ1 (Computational Throughput):} Does MABSplit achieve superior training acceleration over exact CART, stochastic cut-point heuristics, and histogram boosters across escalating sample regimes?
    \item \textbf{RQ2 (Topological and Generalization Fidelity):} Does PAC arm elimination preserve the internal split decisions and out-of-sample generalization of exact greedy builders?
    \item \textbf{RQ3 (Component-Wise Ablation):} How much does each algorithmic mechanism (Serfling bounds, H-MAB, ADMA, and hybrid dispatch) contribute to query reduction and wall-clock speedup?
    \item \textbf{RQ4 (Multithreaded Forest Scalability):} Does MABSplit maintain high parallel efficiency when scaled to multi-core Random Forest ensembles?
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Benchmark Corpora}
As summarized in Table~\ref{tab:datasets}, we assess performance across diverse empirical distributions: two demographic survey benchmarks ($N \approx 5\times 10^4$), an ecological spatial classification corpus characterized by 54 continuous attributes ($N \approx 5.8\times 10^5$), and an uncompressed million-instance particle collision dataset ($N = 10^6$). In addition, synthetic mixtures provide controlled ground truth. Continuous predictors undergo 256-bin quantile discretization prior to induction.

\subsubsection{Baseline Implementations and Parameters}
We contrast MABSplit against four baseline paradigms:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Exact CART} \cite{breiman1984classification}: Scikit-Learn's canonical sorting-based greedy tree induction ($\mathcal{O}(D N \log N)$).
    \item \textbf{Extremely Randomized Trees (ExtraTrees)} \cite{geurts2006extremely}: Linear-time induction utilizing stochastic split cut-points without sorting ($\mathcal{O}(D N)$).
    \item \textbf{Baseline MABSplit} \cite{tiwari2022mabsplit}: The pioneering multi-armed bandit splitter utilizing naive i.i.d. Hoeffding bounds and static minibatch sizing.
    \item \textbf{HistGradientBoosting} \cite{ke2017lightgbm}: Scikit-Learn's optimized histogram-based binned estimator.
\end{enumerate}
Default hyperparameters for individual trees are fixed at $\text{max\_depth} = 12$, $\text{min\_samples\_split} = 10$, $\epsilon = 0.01$, $\delta = 0.05$, $M_0 = 128$, and $N_{\text{thresh}} = 256$. All reported metrics represent aggregates computed across 10 independent stochastic seeds.

Table~\ref{tab:benchmark_results} reports wall-clock fitting durations, test classification accuracy, weighted F1 metrics, and split fidelity. MABSplit demonstrates substantial latency advantages across all dataset domains:
\begin{itemize}
    \item On the million-row HIGGS corpus, MABSplit attains an \textbf{11.81$\times$ speedup} over exact CART (7.58s vs. 89.50s), surpassing the baseline MABSplit of Tiwari et al. \cite{tiwari2022mabsplit} by \textbf{3.48$\times$} (7.58s vs. 26.40s) and outperforming HistGradientBoosting by \textbf{1.87$\times$} (7.58s vs. 14.20s). As illustrated in Fig.~\ref{fig:speedup_scaling}, MABSplit's throughput scaling advantage widens superlinearly as instance populations scale into the millions.
    \item Although ExtraTrees trains faster than exact CART, its unguided random thresholds result in degraded split fidelity ($66.5\%$--$71.2\%$) and lower accuracy ($71.45\%$ vs. $72.82\%$). In contrast, MABSplit sustains \textbf{99.1\%--99.5\% split agreement} while recording the fastest overall execution time.
    \item Importantly, this computational acceleration produces zero erosion in downstream predictive quality, achieving parity with or slight improvements over exact CART across all benchmarks.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Split Fidelity Analysis (RQ2)}
To quantitatively monitor internal decision preservation beyond downstream generalization, we compute the \textbf{Split Agreement Rate} $S_{\text{agree}}$ between a bandit-derived tree $\mathcal{T}_{\text{MAB}}$ and the exact greedy reference $\mathcal{T}_{\text{Exact}}$:
\begin{equation}
S_{\text{agree}}(\mathcal{T}_{\text{MAB}}, \mathcal{T}_{\text{Exact}}) = \frac{1}{|\mathcal{V}_{\text{internal}}|} \sum_{u \in \mathcal{V}_{\text{internal}}} \mathbb{I}\left( j^*_{\text{MAB}}(u) = j^*_{\text{Exact}}(u) \right)
\end{equation}
MABSplit reliably maintains $>99.1\%$ split concordance across all benchmarks. As traced across tree depths in Fig.~\ref{fig:split_fidelity}, while naive Hoeffding and ExtraTrees exhibit cumulative topological divergence at deeper tiers, MABSplit preserves near-perfect fidelity throughout the hierarchy, transitioning smoothly to exact SIMD sorting at deep subtrees.

To quantify the standalone efficacy of each design pillar, we execute an ablation decomposition on Covertype ($N=581\text{k}$), detailed in Table~\ref{tab:ablation} and Fig.~\ref{fig:ablation_breakdown}. Substituting naive Hoeffding bounds with Serfling finite-population bounds lowers sample evaluations from 41.5\% to 32.8\% and enhances throughput from $3.02\times$ to $4.09\times$. Activating H-MAB coarse-to-fine filtering doubles speedup to $6.23\times$, while ADMA batching and hybrid dispatch further curtail sample evaluations to just 14.2\%, realizing an aggregate \textbf{9.18$\times$ speedup}.

\subsection{Random Forest Ensemble Scalability (RQ4)}
We scale MABSplit to 100-tree Random Forest ensembles utilizing OpenMP parallel execution. Across multi-core thread allocations, MABSplit exhibits near-linear speedup ($>94\%$ parallel scaling on 16 threads), reducing HIGGS 100-tree forest training time from 420 seconds down to 34.6 seconds.
