\section{Conclusion and Future Work}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This work demonstrates that the sample complexity of decision tree induction can be reduced from $\mathcal{O}(N)$ to sublinear logarithmic bounds without topological distortion or statistical relaxation. By formalizing tree split evaluation under finite-population empirical Bernstein bounds with the Serfling finite population correction factor, \textbf{MABSplit} eliminates the statistical mismatch, sample inflation, and tie-breaking stalls endemic to infinite-superpopulation bandit splitters. Coupling this bound with hierarchical coarse-to-fine filtering (H-MAB), adaptive dynamic minibatch scaling (ADMA), and a leaf-level hybrid SIMD solver yields up to an $11.8\times$ training speedup over exact Scikit-Learn CART while sustaining $>99.1\%$ split fidelity.

Promising avenues for future inquiry include extending finite-population arm elimination to GPU-resident tensor frameworks, formulating second-order gradient boosted decision trees (MAB-GBDT) using gradient-variance bounds, and exploring nonparametric survival analysis split criteria.
