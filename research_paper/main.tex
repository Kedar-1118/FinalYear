\begin{abstract}
Recursive partitioning in Decision Trees and ensemble models faces severe scalability bottlenecks due to the computational cost of evaluating impurity metrics across extensive continuous candidate thresholds. While framing split optimization as Multi-Armed Bandit (MAB) Best Arm Identification reduces query complexity via subsampling, prevailing formulations invoke infinite-superpopulation bounds that misrepresent without-replacement partitioning and stall under terminal tie-breaking. This paper introduces \textbf{MABSplit}, an accelerated, PAC-optimal induction architecture founded on four structural pillars: (1) \textbf{Serfling finite-population empirical Bernstein bounds} that integrate the $(1 - (n-1)/N)$ correction factor, strictly collapsing uncertainty to zero upon partition exhaustion; (2) \textbf{Hierarchical Coarse-to-Fine Arm Screening (H-MAB)} governed by a formal Bonferroni budget split ($\delta/3$ coarse, $2\delta/3$ fine); (3) \textbf{Adaptive Dynamic Minibatch Allocation (ADMA)} that dynamically scales batch steps as active candidate pools contract; and (4) a \textbf{Depth-Adaptive Hybrid Solver} that routes terminal subtrees to SIMD exact evaluation, avoiding statistical bookkeeping overhead on leaves. Rigorous theoretical proofs confirm global $(\epsilon, \delta)$-PAC optimality with bounded finite-population sample complexity. Extensive empirical evaluations across diverse tabular benchmarks demonstrate \textbf{3.2$\times$--11.8$\times$ wall-clock speedups} over exact Scikit-Learn CART, outperforming baseline bandit splitters by over $3.4\times$ while rigorously preserving $>99.1\%$ internal split agreement and matching baseline generalization.
\end{abstract}
