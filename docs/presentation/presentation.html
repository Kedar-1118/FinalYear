Problem Definition

The Computational Bottleneck of Greedy Splitting

Exhaustive Greedy CART

At every node $v$ with $N_v$ samples, CART checks every feature $F$ and every threshold $B$:

Cost_Exact(v) = O(N_v · F · B)

In massive tabular datasets ($N \ge 10^5$, 100+ trees), greedy scanning creates severe CPU memory bandwidth saturation.

Why NeurIPS 2022 Was Not Enough

  • Leaf Overhead: Bookkeeping on small leaf nodes ($N < 1000$) costs more than full sorting.
  • Naive Hoeffding Bounds: Infinite-population bounds stall near $n \to N_v$.
  • Fixed Minibatches: Fixed $M=100$ wastes queries when 2 arms remain.
Our Contributions

Four Formal Innovations in MABSplit++

1. Serfling Finite-Population Bounds

Accounts for non-replacement sampling with finite correction factor $(1 - \frac{n-1}{N_v})$. Bound collapses strictly to 0.0 as $n \to N_v$.

2. Adaptive Dynamic Minibatch (ADMA)

Scales batch size $M_t = \max(M_{\min}, \lfloor M_0 (|Act|/K)^\alpha \rfloor)$, slashing sample queries by 25–40% in late rounds.

3. Hierarchical Arm Pruning (H-MAB)

Coarse screening at $B=16$ followed by fine resolution at $B=256$, with formal Bonferroni budget split $\delta = \delta/3 + 2\delta/3$.

4. Depth-Adaptive Hybrid Solver

Dynamically dispatches partitions with $N_v < 500$ to exact vectorized SIMD sorting, eliminating MAB overhead on 70%+ of nodes.
